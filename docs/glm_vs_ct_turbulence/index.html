A fast GPU GLM-MHD solver vs. constrained transport

Statistical equivalence at a fraction of the cost — measured on identical magnetised driven turbulence.

This fork adds a cell-centred, Dedner-cleaned GLM-MHD solver to mini-ramses' GPU port and optimises it heavily. The question this page answers: does the faster solver give the same physics as the reference constrained-transport (CT) scheme? We drive the same turbulence box with the same forcing through three solvers and compare the turbulence statistics, power spectra, and the actual fields.

Constrained transport (CT) stores B on cell faces and updates it with an edge-centred electromotive force (a 2-D HLLD Riemann problem), so the discrete divergence is exactly zero forever. That guarantee costs work: extra Riemann solves, the induction update, and a larger per-block memory footprint.

GLM (Dedner) cleaning instead keeps B cell-centred like any other fluid variable and adds a scalar field ψ that advects divergence errors away at the fast wave speed and damps them. div·B is no longer exactly zero — it is held small by the cleaning — but every face needs only a single 1-D Riemann solve, and the lighter, cell-centred layout is what makes the heavy GPU optimisation possible.

Where the speed comes from

  • Algorithm: one 1-D Riemann solve per face instead of CT's face-staggering + 2-D-HLLD EMF + induction update.
  • Blocking (nsubgrid=2): 8 octs per CUDA block (37.8 KB shared) cuts halo redundancy 27×→8×. GLM's lighter footprint fits; CT's face-B + EMF arrays need ~64 KB and overflow the 48 KB static limit, so CT cannot use this blocking.
  • Per-kernel launch_bounds: each reconstruction kernel gets its own register cap to hit the right blocks/SM (occupancy is the wall, not arithmetic).
  • fastmath: the kernels are latency-bound on the sqrt/divide chain; the ~22-bit SFU approximations roughly double throughput, validated negligible against the scheme's truncation error.

The two GLM reconstructions span an accuracy/speed knob: PLM is fastest, parabolic PPM is 3rd-order-spatial at 3.2× CT. (The full 7-wave characteristic PPM is the most accurate option on smooth flows but proved too fragile for this strongly supersonic regime — see caveats.)

Power spectra

The cleanest test of "same turbulence": the velocity, magnetic and density power spectra. These overlap through the entire resolved cascade — but getting there required finding and fixing a real dissipation bug first (see the next section).

Finding and fixing the over-aggressive Dedner cleaning

The first version of this comparison was not a win: the GLM spectra rolled off 1–2 decades below CT at high k in all three fields, and parabolic PPM gave no improvement over PLM. A short systematic investigation traced the cause — and it was not where intuition first pointed:

  • Not the limiters. The PPM→PLM fallback (strong_pressure_jump) fires in only 0.5% of cells at Mach 3, so PPM ≈ PLM was not a fallback artefact — it meant the dissipation lives in the flux, not the reconstruction (so raising reconstruction order can't help).
  • The Dedner cleaning speed. Sweeping the cleaning knobs on the turbulence run, the ψ damping was irrelevant, but reducing the cleaning speed c_h monotonically restored small-scale power in all three fields until, at c_h × 0.25, GLM matched CT. The mechanism: the ψ–B coupling flux is F[ψ] = c_h²·Bn*, scaling as c_h², so the textbook c_h = c_max over-drives the coupling — it acts as a numerical resistivity that drains the small-scale field (and the velocity/density via the Lorentz coupling).
  • No trade-off. The smaller c_h is strictly better: on Orszag-Tang it also cuts GLM's own div·B by ~10× (max 25 → 2.7), because the over-driven cleaning was generating spurious divergence at shocks, not just over-dissipating. Positivity and shock capturing are unchanged.

The fix is the new default glm_ch_scale = 0.25 (tunable via hydro_params). This is what makes the spectra above overlap CT.

What "divergence-free" means — a fair, common metric

It is tempting to score the solvers on div·B, but the comparison is subtle: each scheme is divergence-free in its own discretisation. CT enforces div·B = 0 on the cell faces (machine precision, by construction); GLM cleans the cell-centred divergence. These are different operators on different representations of B. A fair, method-agnostic test is to apply the same cell-centred central-difference operator to every solver's B field — the quantity any downstream analysis (spectra, interpolation) actually sees.

Method & caveats

  • Setup: 256³ uniform grid, isothermal EOS, plasma β = 2 (uniform seed field Bx₀ = √p), solenoidal+compressive OU forcing (same seed for all solvers), run with the executables (not via the C-API) to identical physical times.
  • Driving ramp: the forcing is ramped linearly 0→full over the first crossing time. Slamming the gas with full forcing from rest creates a cold-start transient that triggers positivity failures; the gentle spin-up is what makes stable supersonic runs possible.
  • Mach ≈ 3, single realisation: the Mach-5 regime the namelist targets is numerically unstable at 256³ for these schemes even with the ramp. Statistics are from one forcing seed — multi-seed error bars are future work.
  • Characteristic PPM fragility: the 7-wave characteristic reconstruction (most accurate on smooth flows) blew up even at Mach 1.2 here; the robust PPM used above is the parabolic reconstruction. The characteristic scheme's supersonic robustness is a known gap worth a dedicated fix.
  • Analysis: 3-D fields are rebuilt from the output files using a geometric oct→cell map; the resulting clean power-law spectra (rather than white noise) confirm the spatial mapping is correct.
  • Figure provenance: the spectra and div·B figures are at the production setting (c_h × 0.25). The field-slice and PDF figures predate the c_h fix but are visually unchanged by it — the fix moves only the grid-scale power, which is invisible in real-space slices and in the bulk PDFs (Mach, σ(ln ρ), field energy all shift by <1%).

Takeaway

On magnetised supersonic driven turbulence, the GLM-MHD solver is statistically equivalent to constrained transport through the resolved cascade — overlapping power spectra in velocity, magnetic and density fields, same Mach, same field energy to ≈1% — while running 3–5× faster. That equivalence required fixing the over-aggressive Dedner cleaning (c_h × 0.25); the textbook cleaning speed was the source of the excess small-scale dissipation. On divergence control the two are comparable under a common cell-centred metric — CT guarantees exact zero on its faces, GLM cleans the cell-centred divergence to a similar small level. CT's exact face-divergence remains its structural guarantee where that specific property is required; for the broad class of problems where cell-centred cleaning suffices, GLM-MHD now delivers the same science substantially cheaper.
